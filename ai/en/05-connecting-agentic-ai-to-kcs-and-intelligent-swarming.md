As agentic AI capabilities rapidly emerge, we are in a continuous cycle of discovery and experimentation, testing what’s possible, refining what works, and rethinking how intelligence is designed into the fabric of everyday work.

[Watch “Connecting Agentic AI to KCS® and Intelligent Swarming®” on YouTube](https://youtu.be/e6NqDAF65ok?si=0hCtQ3cEFo67zubq)

In a three-part Consortium for Service Innovation Member-only webinar series presented by [Jeff Elser, Senior Product Manager of Content Intelligence at Oracle](https://blogs.oracle.com/authors/jeff-elser/), attendees gathered for sessions that went beyond surface-level discussions of AI. These conversations explored how knowledge management is evolving into something broader and more strategic: Content Intelligence. The ideas presented in this session are helping Consortium Members shape how they think about AI, value delivery, and the future of work.

At a high level, the sessions focused on one central theme – that AI agents are only as effective as the content, practices, and governance that support them. Here are some of the key takeaways.

## Moving Beyond Knowledge Management

A mindset shift is happening. Knowledge management, long associated primarily with customer service, is expanding rapidly through the enterprise. Content fuels AI agents throughout the business in areas like procurement, policy enforcement, training, and operations. Knowledge is now a strategic asset that enables smarter automation and better decisions across the organization.

## AI Agents are Workers, Not Tools

AI agents are participants in work, not just technology behind the scenes. But they’re not equivalent to humans. Agents can handle routine decisions, identify patterns humans might miss, and collaborate with both people and other agents. Humans remain essential for managing ambiguity, risk, ethics, and exceptions. The value lies in designing systems where agents and humans swarm together effectively.

## Applying KCS Principles in New Ways

Knowledge-Centered Service (KCS) Principles are more relevant than ever in this AI-driven world. Trust, demand-driven content, reuse, and validated knowledge are just as critical for AI agents as they are for people. KCS Practices can guide agent behavior, reduce variability in AI outputs, and improve consistency and quality over time.

All of the [KCS Principles](https://library.serviceinnovation.org/KCS/KCS_v6/KCS_Principles_and_Core_Concepts) still apply. Trust is a big one… knowledge grounds AI and that grounding fosters trust in AI agent output.

*– Jeff Elser, Oracle*

## Measuring Value, Not Just Activity

Measuring success in an AI-enabled enterprise goes beyond traditional metrics like citation rates or content volume. We evaluate performance by looking at the outcome. How are agents using content? What decisions are being reused? Where is value being created or degraded? These questions point to a more mature approach to measurement that aligns content, AI, and business results.

## Why This Matters

The mix of deep experience with AI and firsthand experience with how knowledge-driven organizations work makes these sessions especially valuable as the world transforms around us. Jeff connects agentic AI to real-world challenges, existing assumptions, and explores ideas that are still emerging. The result is shared thinking that helps Consortium Members stay ahead of change and apply AI in ways that are practical, defensible, and aligned with their organizations’ goals.
